There is no single global GxP regulation. GxP is a family of good practice rules (GMP, GCP, GLP, GDP and others) that regulators in each jurisdiction write, enforce and inspect against, and they converge on the same principles: controlled processes, qualified people, validated systems and records you can trust. The practical task for a multi-region team is to build one evidence model that satisfies all of them. Qualitum's platform is built around that idea: agent-authored validation records that are ALCOA+ checked at write-time and review-time, so the same evidence stands up in front of an FDA, EMA or MHRA inspector.

## TL;DR

- GxP is shorthand for a set of good practices. GLP, GCP and GMP are always part of the minimum, and GDP, GVP and good documentation practice are added by jurisdiction.
- The US, EU, UK, Canada and Japan share principles but differ in legal instruments, effective dates and how binding their guidance is.
- Computerized systems and data integrity are where the frameworks meet: 21 CFR Part 11, EU Annex 11, GAMP 5 and ALCOA+ all ask for risk-based validation and trustworthy records.
- Inspection readiness is a state you maintain through traceable evidence, not a project you start when an audit is announced.

## GxP is a family of practices, not one regulation

The "x" stands for the activity. [EUPATI describes GxP](https://toolbox.eupati.eu/resources/good-practice-gxp/) as generic good practice: the laws, regulations and guidance governing research, development, testing, manufacturing and distribution of medicines. Its [course on key GxPs](https://learning.eupati.eu/mod/book/tool/print/index.php?id=661) lists GLP, GCP, GMP, GVP, GDP and good documentation practice (GDocP) as the most relevant, and states that GLP, GCP and GMP are always included as a minimum, while the full set depends on jurisdiction. Some GxPs sit inside others. Good documentation practice, for example, is subsumed under GMP.

The table below maps each practice to what it governs and to a key US reference, using the [CASRAI GxP compliance guide](https://casrai.org/guides/gxp-compliance).

| Practice | What it governs | Key US reference |
|---|---|---|
| GLP | Non-clinical safety studies | 21 CFR Part 58 |
| GCP | Human clinical trials | ICH E6(R3), 21 CFR Parts 312 and 812 |
| GMP | Manufacturing and quality systems | 21 CFR Parts 210 and 211, ICH Q7 for APIs |
| GDP | Wholesale distribution | FDA drug supply chain requirements (EU: GDP guidelines 2013/C 343/01) |
| GVP | Pharmacovigilance | 21 CFR 314.80 (EU: EMA GVP modules) |

Two clarifications save teams from over-scoping. GAMP is an ISPE industry framework, not a regulation, and ordinary academic or basic research is not GxP-regulated, according to the same guide.

## Why location of the site does not limit the rules

GxP obligations follow the product and the market, not the factory's postal address. The EMA states that any manufacturer of medicines [intended for the EU market must comply with EU GMP](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/human-regulatory-overview/research-development/compliance-research-development/good-manufacturing-practice) no matter where in the world it is located. The same logic applies to clinical data: a company applying for an EU marketing authorisation must ensure that all trials in the application adhere to EU-recognised GCP principles, [regardless of where the trials were conducted](https://toolbox.eupati.eu/resources/good-practice-gxp/).

For a US-based team supplying Europe, that means two inspection regimes can apply to one site. The EU legal framework for GMP is a stack of instruments: Regulation 1252/2014 for active substances, Directive 2001/83/EC and Directive (EU) 2017/1572 for human medicines, and Directive 2001/20/EC and Regulation (EU) 536/2014 for investigational products, with EU GMP guidelines and annexes giving the interpretation. The EMA's GMP page lists them all.

## What every jurisdiction expects: the shared core

Under the different legal wrappers, the expectations are strikingly similar. The EUPATI course summarises the GMP principles as:

- adequate, clean facilities with restricted access and controlled environments;
- defined and validated critical processes;
- calibrated and maintained equipment;
- evaluated changes;
- clear procedures and qualified, trained employees;
- documented and archived processes, per SOPs;
- correct materials, containers and labels;
- investigated and documented deviations.

The [WHO GMP guide (TRS 986 Annex 2)](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/medicines/norms-and-standards/guidelines/inspections/trs986annex2.pdf) adds the management layer. Senior management carries ultimate responsibility for an adequately resourced pharmaceutical quality system. That system should include supplier selection and monitoring, calibrations and validations, self-inspection and quality audit procedures, deviation investigation with root-cause analysis, CAPA effectiveness monitoring and continual improvement.

Note the phrase "performance-based". EUPATI points out that GMP standards are not prescriptive cookbook instructions. You decide how to meet them, and you must be able to show why your approach is fit for purpose. That is why validation evidence carries so much weight in every region.

## Where jurisdictions differ: instruments, dates and binding force

The differences are mostly about timing and legal status. The clearest recent example is ICH E6(R3), the international GCP guideline. According to the [CASRAI adoption table](https://casrai.org/guides/ich-e6r3-audit-inspection-monitoring-definitions):

- ICH finalised the Principles and Annex 1 on 6 January 2025, and Annex 2 (pragmatic, decentralised and real-world-data trials) on 3 June 2026.
- In the EU, the Principles and Annex 1 have been the operative GCP guideline since 23 July 2025. Annex 2 was adopted by CHMP on 25 June 2026 and takes effect on 15 January 2027.
- The US FDA posted its final guidance on 8 September 2025. It is non-binding and has no fixed compliance date.
- Health Canada's adoption took effect on 1 April 2026, with a transition to 30 September 2026 and full compliance from 1 October 2026.

The same source notes that E6(R3) lowers the threshold at which authorities may seek audit reports, to cases of evidence or suspicion of serious GCP noncompliance. For clinical research managers, that is a reason to keep audit documentation inspection-grade at all times.

Data integrity shows a different kind of gap. A March 2026 Japanese industry document on electronic-information requirements states that [Japan has issued no domestic data-integrity requirement](https://www.jpma.or.jp/information/evaluation/results/allotment/nmeom8000000fgfb-att/EI_20260302_ereshb.pdf), while FDA, MHRA and WHO requirements exist overseas and the basic principles are ALCOA-CCEA. A company filing in Japan therefore still needs to meet the principles its other markets expect. Nothing in a single-country program covers that automatically.

UK expectations are covered in our guide to [MHRA data integrity for US teams](https://blog.qualitum.ai/mhra-data-integrity).

## Computerized systems: where the frameworks meet

For validation teams this is the busiest intersection. Four references recur in almost every inspection of a computerized system.

**21 CFR Part 11.** The FDA's scope and application guidance says Part 11 applies to [records in electronic form that are created, modified, maintained, archived, retrieved or transmitted](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/part-11-electronic-records-electronic-signatures-scope-and-application) under records requirements in FDA regulations. The same guidance describes a narrow interpretation of scope, so the first job is deciding which records are in scope under the underlying predicate rules.

**Computer software assurance.** The FDA's final guidance of 24 September 2025 describes computer software assurance as a [risk-based, least-burdensome approach](https://ispewebassets.org/files/regulatory/2025/guidance-computer-software-assurance-production-quality-system.pdf) to establishing confidence that software used in device production or the quality system is fit for intended use and stays validated throughout its life cycle. It explicitly covers cloud models (IaaS, PaaS, SaaS). It addresses medical-device production, but the risk-based reasoning is useful well beyond it.

**GAMP 5.** The [second edition, published in July 2022](https://guidance-docs.ispe.org/doi/book/10.1002/9781946964571), provides a risk-based framework for computerized systems that are fit for intended use and compliant. It updates the first edition for the growing role of service providers, iterative software development and automation tools, and stresses critical thinking by experienced subject-matter experts. Our [practical GAMP 5 validation guide](https://blog.qualitum.ai/gamp-5-validation) and the piece on [GAMP 5's risk-based approach](https://blog.qualitum.ai/gamp-5-risk-based) go through the lifecycle.

**ALCOA+.** Citing PIC/S PI 041-1 (July 2021), EUPATI sets out data-integrity principles for paper and electronic records alike. Records must be Attributable (who did what and when, including changes), Legible, Contemporaneous (recorded as the action happens), Complete, Consistent, Enduring and Available. For a deeper walk through each attribute, see [what each ALCOA+ principle requires](https://blog.qualitum.ai/alcoa-compliance), and for the record types that trip teams up, [static versus dynamic data](https://blog.qualitum.ai/static-vs-dynamic-data).

## Inspection readiness across regions

The FDA's [CGMP compliance program](https://www.fda.gov/media/75167/download?_sp=2e2d7d16-ec34-41b2-8014-857d887ec04e) says inspections focus on system-wide controls: quality, facilities and equipment, materials, production, packaging and labeling, and laboratory controls. FDA expects compliant establishments to operate in a state of control. The quality system under 21 CFR 211.22 should provide senior management oversight, quality policies, quality planning, resource management and management review, using knowledge management and quality risk management consistent with ICH Q9, Q10 and Q12.

WHO's guide describes the same state of control through effective monitoring of process performance and product quality. Different words, same test: can you show, from records, that your processes are controlled right now?

That points to three habits for a multi-region quality unit:

1. **Keep a live link from requirement to evidence.** Every URS line should trace to a test and its result, so a question from any inspector resolves in minutes.
2. **Check records when they are written, not when the audit is announced.** An ALCOA+ gap found at write-time costs a correction. Found during an inspection, it becomes an observation.
3. **Keep human accountability visible.** Signatures, approvals and review decisions need a clear owner, whatever tooling produced the draft.

## A working approach for a multi-region program

Start with a requirements map rather than a binder per country. List each GxP that applies to your products (GMP, GCP, GLP, GDP, GVP), the jurisdictions you file or supply in, and the instrument for each. Mark where a regulation is binding and where guidance is non-binding, as with FDA's E6(R3) guidance. Then align your quality system to the strictest common denominator for the principles, and handle local deltas as documented exceptions.

![Steps for mapping multi-region GxP requirements](https://media.babylovegrowth.ai/blog-images/organization-48457/1795269767661_Steps-for-mapping-multi-region-GxP-requirements.jpeg)

Next, make validation the engine rather than the bottleneck. Most of the delay in a validation cycle is authoring: URS, risk assessments, IQ/OQ/PQ protocols, execution review and traceability. It is also where inconsistent records creep in. Finally, decide where your data lives. ## FAQ

### Is there one global GxP regulation?

No. GxP is a collective term for good practices, and each jurisdiction issues its own laws and guidance. ICH guidelines such as E6 for GCP and Q7 for APIs harmonize expectations, but regional authorities decide how and when they apply, as the E6(R3) adoption dates in the US, EU and Canada show.

### Which GxPs are the minimum?

According to EUPATI, GLP, GCP and GMP are always included as a minimum. Whether GDP, GVP and good documentation practice are added as separate requirements depends on the jurisdiction.

### Does a US manufacturer have to follow EU GMP?

If the medicines are intended for the EU market, yes. The EMA states that manufacturers must comply with EU GMP no matter where in the world they are located. Many multi-market companies run one quality system that meets both sets of expectations.

### Is GAMP 5 a regulation?

No. GAMP 5 is an ISPE industry framework offering a risk-based approach to computerized system compliance. Regulators generally accept it as good practice, and it pairs naturally with Part 11 and Annex 11 expectations.

### What does ALCOA+ have to do with global requirements?

It is the common vocabulary of data integrity. PIC/S guidance applies it to paper and electronic records, and FDA, MHRA and WHO expectations are built on the same principles, so records that meet it travel across inspections.
